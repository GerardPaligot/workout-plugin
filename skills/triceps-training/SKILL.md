# Triceps training

## Triceps anatomy and actions

The triceps has **two main actions**:

1. **Elbow extension**: straightening the arm (primary action).
2. **Shoulder extension (retropulsion)**: bringing the arm backward (involvement of the long head).

For complete stimulation, an exercise that combines these two actions will be superior to one that covers only one.

## Exercise ranking (worst to best)

### Avoid

#### Dumbbell kickback
- **Stability**: poor (torso bent over, unstable).
- **Progressive overload**: impossible to use heavy loads in a strict manner.
- **Range of motion**: limited.
- You always tend to cheat with inertia and other muscle groups.
- It is a high contraction tension exercise, but much better alternatives exist for that.

#### Close-grip bench press
- Loads the **anterior deltoid** and the pectorals more than the triceps.
- Hard to emphasize elbow extension → the emphasis is on shoulder flexion (role of the anterior deltoid).
- Compound exercise but poorly aligned with the triceps' primary action.
- Modifying it by lowering the bar toward the throat (California press) can improve it, but as such: replace it with dips.

#### Supinated extensions
- The triceps is **not responsible** for supination (unlike the biceps).
- Supination puts the grip at a disadvantage → lower loads → limited progressive overload.
- Wrecks the wrists.
- The idea that supination isolates the "vastus medialis" (medial head) is nonsense.
- **Rule**: choose pronated or neutral grip for ALL triceps exercises.

### Good exercise

#### Overhead extensions (French press / overhead triceps extension)
- **High stretch tension** exercise → high hypertrophic potential.
- Perfect path and resistance curve.
- The long head of the triceps is heavily loaded.
- Weak point: **setup and stability** can be complicated, especially without an adjustable cable.
- A seat can improve stability but makes the setup even more complex.
- **Advanced variation**: dual cable, both hands crossed behind the head ("samurai") → perfect path, optimal stimulation (but the setup is even more complex).

### Best exercises

#### Dips (machine or bench edge) — 2nd place
- **Warning**: bench-edge dips or machine dips for the triceps. Not parallel-bar dips (those are more for the chest).
- Exploits the **length-tension relationship**: shortens the triceps at the elbow (extension) while lengthening it at the shoulder → fibers always in the optimal position for force production (see the `lifting-biomechanics` skill).
- Excellent progressive overload possible.
- On the machine: near-perfect stability.
- On the edge of a bench (old-school style with plates on the thighs): very effective, little equipment needed.
- EMG studies show one of the best muscle activations for this movement.
- Don't let your ego take over despite the ability to load heavy.

#### Cheated extensions (dumbbells/barbell) — 1st place
- **The best exercise** for the triceps. Combines two phases:
  1. **Elbow flexion**: bring the dumbbells/barbell down to forehead level (like a skull crusher).
  2. **Shoulder extension**: take it far back behind the head to stretch the long head (like a pullover).
  3. Then return in the opposite direction: bring the elbows back to chest level + elbow extension.
- Covers the triceps' **two actions** (elbow extension + retropulsion) in a single movement.
- Provides both high stretch tension AND some contraction tension.
- Lying on a bench → excellent stability.
- Allows **intensification techniques** (e.g. mechanical drop set).
- Versatile: short or long sets, metabolic or mechanical stress.
- **Tip**: start with light dumbbells to get familiar with the path (2 distinct phases), then increase gradually.

## Minimal triceps program

| Exercise | Type | Sets |
|---|---|---|
| Cheated extensions | Stretch + contraction | 3-4 |
| Dips (machine/bench) | Compound, optimal length-tension | 3-4 |
| Overhead extensions | High stretch tension | 3-4 |

With these 3 exercises: complete work from stretch to contraction, good stability, progressive overload possible, good resistance curve.
