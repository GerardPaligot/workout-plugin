# Biceps training

## Biceps anatomy and actions

The biceps has **two main actions**:

1. **Elbow flexion**: bending the arm (primary action).
2. **Supination**: turning the palm upward (the biceps is a supinator, unlike the triceps).

The biceps has two heads:
- **Short head** (inner).
- **Long head** (outer) — crosses the shoulder joint, which makes it sensitive to arm position.

### Biceps particularity

The biceps is a **very weak muscle when contracted**. That is why exercises with high contraction tension (e.g. spider curl) can be worthwhile to strengthen this position.

## Exercise ranking (worst to best)

### Avoid

#### Drag curl
- **Minimal** range of motion.
- Unstable position → tendency to compensate with the trapezius.
- Turns the movement into a poorly controlled compound exercise.
- In theory exploits the length-tension relationship, but execution conditions are too poor to benefit from it.
- Source's view: with a heavier load it turns into a shrug (the shoulders do the second half of the rep).
- Range never allows a full stretch of both heads → no strong stretch tension. Free weight resists along the vertical line of force (see the `lifting-biomechanics` skill).

#### EZ-bar curl
- The EZ bar encourages **narrowing the hands** and **flaring the elbows** → humerus on a diagonal instead of aligned with gravity.
- The elbow joint can only bend and extend → a diagonal humerus causes suboptimal work + elbow pain in the long run.
- Tucking the elbows in to correct this can worsen pain if there is a pronounced elbow valgus.
- **Alternative**: unilateral dumbbell curl → lets you compensate for the valgus by leaning to the side, better wrist comfort, maximal supination possible.

### Good exercise

#### Spider curl
- **High contraction tension** exercise → very easy to feel.
- Can provide **constant tension** if you don't go too low and keep the humeri perpendicular to the floor.
- Worthwhile for strengthening the biceps in its contracted position (where it is naturally weak).
- Weak point: insufficient **stability**. When loaded, tendency to pull the elbows backward → turns it into a compound exercise → loses the benefit of isolation.
- The preacher curl (Scott curl) offers more stability but has a different resistance curve (not really high contraction tension).
- Both movements are good in specific cases.

### Best exercises

#### Incline curl (dumbbells) — 3rd place
- **High stretch tension** exercise → very effective for hypertrophy.
- You can **adjust the bench incline** according to your joint mobility and the length of your biceps.
- Can be done with both arms or unilaterally.
- **Incline progression**: start more upright, then gradually lower the bench angle over the weeks/months to get more stretch (same logic as the knees traveling forward in the squat).
- Be careful if you have a short biceps: adapt the range of motion (don't force full extension) and the incline to avoid the risk of a tear.

#### Supinated-grip pull-ups (chin-ups) — 2nd place
- The only truly worthwhile **compound** exercise for the biceps (the drag curl doesn't count).
- Exploits the **length-tension relationship**: shortens the biceps at the elbow while lengthening it at the shoulder → fibers always in the optimal position for force production (see the `lifting-biomechanics` skill).
- Potentially very **heavy** working load → high mechanical tension.
- For the biceps, it is the equivalent of dips for the triceps.
- Gymnasts develop their biceps mainly through pull-ups.
- **Limitation**: requires mastery of your bodyweight. If you can't string together sets of 10-12 reps, it is less relevant in a biceps program.

#### Cable curl facing the pulley (dual pulley) — 1st place
- **The best exercise** for the biceps. Ticks every box:
  - Excellent **stability**.
  - Easy **progressive overload**.
  - Perfect **biomechanical** consistency.
  - Ideal movement **path**.
  - **Constant tension** (neither the forearm nor the humerus is aligned with the cable at any point).
  - **High stretch tension** exercise.
- Works both the **short head** and the **long head** of the biceps.
- The cable pulls backward → forces the long head (which crosses the shoulder) to contract → potentially beneficial for shoulder joint health.
- **Execution tips**:
  - Stand **upright** (not leaning forward).
  - Keep the **humeri perpendicular to the floor**.
  - Tension must be present **from the very start** of the movement.
  - Don't move the elbows back → otherwise it turns into an incline curl and loses its benefit.
- Can be done **unilaterally** but you lose some stability (resisting the rotation of the weight → obliques engaged). If possible, prefer the bilateral version.
- The cable is the most optimal equipment for the arms (biceps and triceps).

#### Variant: low-cable curl with back to the pulley
- Source's replacement for the drag curl, alongside the incline curl.
- Same arm position as the drag curl, but the cable redirects the resistance → better range of motion and stretch than the free-weight version.
- Not ranked by the source against the exercises above.

## Minimal biceps program

| Exercise | Type | Sets |
|---|---|---|
| Cable curl facing the pulley | High stretch tension + constant tension | 3-4 |
| Supinated-grip pull-ups (chin-ups) | Compound, optimal length-tension | 3-4 |
| Incline curl (optional) | High stretch tension | 3-4 |

Mid-ranking variants (use as needed): spider curl, preacher curl, RX curl, crucifix curl.
