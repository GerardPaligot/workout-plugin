# Shoulder training

## Anatomy: 3 heads, 3 different actions

The deltoid is made up of 3 heads with distinct actions. A complete shoulder program must differentiate between them.

| Head | Location | Main action |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior deltoid | Front of the shoulder | Arm flexion (raising in front) |
| Middle (lateral) deltoid | Side of the shoulder | Arm abduction (raising to the side) |
| Posterior deltoid | Back of the shoulder | Horizontal adduction (pulling the arm backward) + slight external rotation |

Note: according to some sources, the deltoid could be made up of 7 or even 9 specific heads. To keep things simple, we work with the 3 main heads.

### Specifics of the rear delt

- **Common insertion**: the 3 heads of the deltoid converge on the deltoid tuberosity (V-shaped, on the humerus).
- **Origin of the posterior head**: the scapula (shoulder blade).
- **Majority of slow-twitch fibers** → tolerates large training volumes and short rests well (45-60 seconds are enough).
- **Variable fiber path**: depending on morphology, the fibers can be more horizontal or more diagonal → direct impact on the optimal execution angle.
- **Slight external rotation**: the rear delt takes part in external rotation of the humerus → the **neutral grip** (semi-supination) can be more effective than pronation (confirmed by an EMG study).

### Why the rear delt often lags behind

- Many lifters think compound movements (rows, seated row) are enough.
- According to a study (2015), the **reverse pec deck** (single-joint) activates the rear delt more than the seated row or the inclined lat pulldown.
- **You need to add dedicated single-joint movements** for the rear delt on top of the compound ones.

## Exercise ranking (worst to best)

### Avoid

#### Arnold press
- The internal rotation added during the movement is **artificial** and does not improve muscle recruitment.
- The fibers of the middle deltoid are not responsible for internal rotation → this component is useless.
- No advantage over other presses.
- Source's view: execution standard is unclear (some press linearly, others open the arms before pushing), stimulation is not the best for the setup effort, and humerus rotation creates a stability issue. Classic military or overhead press: easier progressive overload.

#### Upright row
- Causes **shoulder joint pain** in the majority of lifters.
- It is not really clear whether it is an exercise for the deltoids or the trapezius.
- Requires internal rotation at the start of the movement → useless for stimulation.
- Brings nothing essential compared with the higher-ranked exercises.
- **Exception**: if you have done it for years without pain, you can continue.
- Source's view (separate video): stopped even though it caused no pain (good feel in deltoids and some trapezius), because it brings nothing clearly better than safer exercises that are as effective or more.

### Good exercises

#### Dumbbell raises (lateral, front, bent-over reverse fly)
- **High contraction tension** exercises (peak tension at the top of the movement, none at the bottom).
- **Single-joint** exercises → little accumulated nervous fatigue.
- Very easy to **feel**, useful if the deltoids are a weak point (can be placed at the start of the session).
- Slightly lower hypertrophic potential than high stretch tension exercises.

#### Cable raises (lateral, front, bent-over reverse fly)
- **Superior** to the dumbbell versions thanks to a better resistance curve.
- The cable lets you synchronize the resistance curve with the muscle's force curve (see the `lifting-biomechanics` skill).
- **Execution tip**: stand **upright** (not leaning to the side) and position your body so the cable is perpendicular to the arm at the middle of the movement.
- Leaning to the side on the cable = removes the benefit of using the cable.

#### Full lateral raise
- Single-joint, relatively light load. Source's view: replaces the upright row.
- Not ranked by the source against the exercises above.
- Hands finish above the shoulders → works the upper trapezius beyond a simple shrug (source: this is why light loads are enough).
- Source credits it with the anterior and middle deltoid early in the movement and the trapezius at the end.
- Suggested placement: first shoulder session.

### Best exercises

#### Overhead press (pressing overhead)
- Works the anterior + middle deltoid + trapezius + triceps.
- Movement that starts **very low** → high stretch tension possible on the shoulders.
- Works the whole **scapular chain**.
- Widely used in Olympic weightlifting (weightlifters have massive deltoids and traps).
- If you only do one exercise for the shoulders → overhead press.

#### Military press
- Typically hypertrophy-oriented execution: vertical path, controlled active range of motion.
- Loads the anterior deltoid well and the middle a bit.
- Less complete than the overhead press but allows better isolation.
- If several exercises are possible → military press + cable raises (bent-over reverse fly for the rear delt).
- Source's view: a guided-machine military press (4 × 8, short rep range, first exercise of the shoulder day) is "exceptional" for the anterior deltoid; he wants a compound press first.

### Summary: importance of vertical presses

Vertical presses (pressing overhead) are **fundamental**. Never train without this type of movement, because not pressing overhead can have a negative influence on other muscle groups (the chest in particular).

- Exercise order within a session: see `training-programming`, "Exercise order within a session (source's view)".

## Minimal shoulder program

| Goal | Suggested exercises |
|---|---|
| Only 1 exercise | Overhead press |
| Complete program | Military press + bent-over cable reverse fly (or cable lateral raise) |

## Rear delt exercises (worst to best)

### Contraction tension

#### Incline bench chest-supported reverse fly (dumbbells)
- Lie **face down** on an incline bench, dumbbells a few centimeters off the floor.
- **Protract the shoulder blades** (let them spread apart) as much as possible at the bottom (rounding the upper back = OK with light loads of 2-4 kg).
- Raise the dumbbells toward the ceiling **without bending the elbows** (single-joint movement).
- The **shoulder blades do not move** from start to finish.
- The bench provides stability → lets you concentrate on the feel.

##### Finding your optimal angle

Test 3 angles between the arm and the torso to identify the one that gives the best rear delt feel:

| Arm/torso angle | Description |
|---|---|
| 90° | Arms perpendicular to the torso |
| 60° | Arms slightly closed |
| 45° | Arms clearly closed |

Do 3 sets with each angle and keep the one that works best. This same angle will be reused on the other exercises.

### Contraction tension (compound)

#### Cable seated row — rear delt focus
- Torso **fully upright**, neutral spine (no arching, no rounding).
- Before pulling: **protract the shoulder blades** (let them spread apart) as much as possible (hands toward the pulley).
- Pull the elbows backward **while keeping the shoulder blades protracted** (hard to master).
- Keep the **same arm/torso angle** identified on the reverse fly.
- The movement seems counter-intuitive, but it provides a greater range of motion and better isolation of the rear delt.
- More effective variation than the classic seated row for the rear delt because it prevents the rhomboids/trapezius from taking over.

### Stretch tension

#### Unilateral cable fly (rear delt)
- Stand at **~45° to the pulley** (pulley almost behind you).
- At the start of the movement (arm against the torso), the cable must be **perpendicular to the humerus** → maximal tension when the rear delt is fully stretched.
- **Half range of motion** only (stretched phase): don't open all the way, otherwise the cable rubs against the body.
- This is the exercise that can be the hardest to master → place it 3rd, after pre-fatiguing and pumping the rear delt.
- Potentially the most effective exercise for making a lagging rear delt "explode".
- Source's view: a high-cable reverse fly is the best rear-delt choice for him ("nothing better"), very adaptable (sets/reps not stated).

### Complementary exercise (anterior + middle)

#### Clean and press
- Combination of a **pull + a press** = complete compound movement.
- Works the **anterior** deltoid and the **middle** deltoid.
- Dynamic execution, chain the reps together.
- Ideal at the end of the shoulder session to complete the work on the 3 heads.

## Rear delt program (dedicated session)

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Tempo | Rest | Tension type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incline bench chest-supported reverse fly | 3 × 20 | 1-0-1-0 | 45-60s | Contraction (single-joint) |
| 2 | Cable seated row, rear delt focus | 4 × 12 | 2-0-1-1 | 45-60s | Contraction (compound) |
| 3 | Unilateral cable fly (rear delt) | 4 × 8 | 2-1-1-0 | Free | Stretch |
| 4 | Clean and press | 1 × 50 (rest-pause) | Dynamic | Rest-pause | Anterior + middle |

### Notes on the rest-pause clean and press

- Load = **bodyweight ÷ 2** (e.g. 80 kg → barbell at 40 kg).
- Do 50 reps, stopping when necessary, without setting the barbell down on the floor.

### Frequency

- Ideal: **3 times every 15 days**.
- Alternative: 2 times per week (the deltoid recovers well).
- Suits a split or hybrid format (chest / back / shoulders / arms / legs) better than push-pull-legs.

## Hand grip for the rear delt

Because the rear delt is responsible for a slight external rotation, the **neutral** or **semi-neutral grip** can be more effective (EMG study). Test the 3 grips (pronated, neutral, supinated) and keep the one that gives the best feel.
